Theoretical Foundation: Do the Two Methods Converge to the Same Value?

The Fundamental Theorem for Riemann-Integrable Functions

A theorem in real analysis states: If a bounded function $f: [a, b] \to \mathbb{R}$ is Riemann-integrable, then it is also Lebesgue-integrable, and the two integrals coincide:

∫ₐᵇ f(x) dx = ∫_{[a, b]} f dμ

Therefore, as the partition sizes tend to zero ($N \to \infty$ and $M \to \infty$), both numerical algorithms are guaranteed to converge to the exact same numerical limit. However:

  • Convergence Speed: Riemann midpoint rule converges quadratically $O(\Delta x^2)$ for smooth functions. Numerical Lebesgue discretizations using level-sets converge linearly $O(\Delta y)$.
  • Computational Cost: Riemann evaluates $f$ directly at $N$ points in $O(N)$ operations. Numerical Lebesgue requires sorting or sampling into bins, costing $O(S + M)$ operations.
  • Scope of Integrability: The reverse is false. Functions like Dirichlet's are Lebesgue-integrable but completely fail under Riemann integration.
