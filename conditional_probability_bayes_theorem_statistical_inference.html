Theoretical Foundations, Worked Examples & Critical Evaluation

Conditional Probability: Probability vs. Statistics

In Kolmogorov's probability theory, for events $A, B \in \mathcal{F}$ with $P(B) > 0$, conditioning restricts the sample space to $B$:

$$P(A \mid B) = \frac{P(A \cap B)}{P(B)}$$

In frequentist statistics, parameters $\theta$ are fixed unknown constants, so probability applies only to data sampling distributions $f(D \mid \theta)$. Bayesian statistics treats $\theta$ as a random variable with epistemic distribution $\pi(\theta)$, allowing Bayes' theorem to invert the conditioning from $f(D \mid \theta)$ to $\pi(\theta \mid D)$.

Bayes' Theorem & The Three Components

Combining the multiplication rule $f(D, \theta) = f(D \mid \theta)\pi(\theta) = \pi(\theta \mid D)m(D)$ yields:

$$\pi(\theta \mid D) = \frac{L(\theta; D)\,\pi(\theta)}{\int_{\Theta} L(\vartheta; D)\,\pi(\vartheta)\,d\vartheta}$$
  • Prior $\pi(\theta)$: Pre-data belief distribution on $\Theta$.
  • Likelihood $L(\theta; D) = f(D \mid \theta)$: Plausibility of $\theta$ given fixed observed data $D$ (not a density in $\theta$).
  • Posterior $\pi(\theta \mid D)$: Normalized product updating belief via evidence $m(D) = \int_\Theta L(\vartheta; D)\pi(\vartheta)\,d\vartheta$.

Coherent Sequential Updating & Worked Examples

When conditionally independent datasets $D_1, D_2$ arrive sequentially, using the first posterior $\pi(\theta \mid D_1)$ as the prior for $D_2$ yields the exact same inference as batch conditioning: $$\pi(\theta \mid D_1, D_2) \propto f(D_2 \mid \theta)\,\pi(\theta \mid D_1) \propto f(D_2 \mid \theta)f(D_1 \mid \theta)\,\pi(\theta) = f(D_1, D_2 \mid \theta)\,\pi(\theta)$$

Example 1: Beta–Binomial Proportion Estimation For $k$ successes in $n$ trials with prior $\text{Beta}(\alpha_0, \beta_0)$, the posterior is $\text{Beta}(\alpha_0 + k, \beta_0 + n - k)$. Its mean is a precision-weighted average: $$\mathbb{E}[\theta \mid D] = \frac{n_0}{n_0 + n}\mu_0 + \frac{n}{n_0 + n}\hat{\theta}_{\text{MLE}}, \quad n_0 = \alpha_0 + \beta_0$$
Example 2: Normal–Normal Precision Updating For observations $\bar{x}$ with known variance $\sigma^2$ and prior $\theta \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu_0, \tau_0^2)$, posterior precision equals prior precision plus data precision: $$\frac{1}{\tau_n^2} = \frac{1}{\tau_0^2} + \frac{n}{\sigma^2}, \qquad \mu_n = \tau_n^2\left(\frac{\mu_0}{\tau_0^2} + \frac{n\bar{x}}{\sigma^2}\right)$$
Advantages of Bayesian Inference Limitations & Methodological Caveats
Direct Probabilistic Statements: Yields a full posterior distribution $\pi(\theta \mid D)$ and credible intervals $P(a \le \theta \le b \mid D) = 0.95$, directly answering questions about parameter uncertainty. Prior Sensitivity in Small Samples: When $n$ is small ($n \lesssim n_0$), subjective or poorly calibrated priors can heavily bias conclusions toward $\mu_0$.
Regularization & Hierarchical Modeling: Informative priors prevent extreme overfitting (e.g., when $k = 0$ or $k = n$ in small samples, avoiding $\hat{\theta}_{\text{MLE}} \in \{0, 1\}$). Cromwell's Rule (Zero-Prior Trap): If $\pi(\theta) = 0$ on a subset of $\Theta$, no finite sample size can ever overturn that zero probability ($0 \times L(\theta; D) = 0$).
Asymptotic Consistency (Bernstein–von Mises): Under suitable regularity conditions, posterior inference becomes increasingly dominated by the data as $n \to \infty$, and the posterior converges to $\mathcal{N}(\hat{\theta}_{\text{MLE}}, \mathcal{I}(\theta_0)^{-1}/n)$. Computational Intractability: Outside conjugate families, evaluating the marginal evidence $m(D) = \int_\Theta L(\vartheta; D)\pi(\vartheta)\,d\vartheta$ in high dimensions requires MCMC or Variational Inference.
